## 1. Executive summary

The Daily Work Tracker replaces the daily stand-up for the XR team. Each team member logs what they worked on that day: tasks, project, status, what changed since yesterday, blockers and evidence (screenshots, files, links). Each member also keeps one open weekly commitment, which the app follows up on until it is delivered. The manager gets a live view of the team: who has submitted, what is blocked, which commitments are slipping, how projects are tracking against deadlines, and an automated summary email every Sunday.

Where it stands on 16 Oct 2026:

- **In daily use.** All 7 registered team members have submitted at least once. Since the week of 31 Aug, 5 to 6 members have been active each week, logging 18 to 28 updates a week.
- **199 daily updates** and **213 task rows** logged. **55 commitments** completed, **29 on time (52.7%)**.
- **12 weekly summary emails** sent automatically.
- **The accountability loop works.** Commitments are tracked, carried forward with a count, and escalated as stalled after 3 carries. Only 1 commitment is currently stalled.
- **Gaps:**
  - Submission rate is about 54% of possible working days, against a 90% target.
  - Manager review is rarely used: 3 of 199 updates have been marked reviewed.
  - Security hardening is still pending. The most serious item is that database row-level security is switched off (section 10).
- **Recommended next step:** a short security and data-integrity phase before any new features (section 15).

## 2. Overview

The app is an internal web application for a Meril Life Sciences engineering and XR team with one manager. It started as a single-file HTML prototype stored in the browser (kept at the workspace root as legacy files). It was rebuilt in June 2026 as a Next.js application backed by Supabase. It has been extended in steps since then: structured tasks and projects, security hardening, the commitments loop, evidence attachments, realtime updates, self-service absence, CSV export, the weekly summary email, and, in September, a simpler weekly-only commitment model.

### Vision
A fast, low-friction replacement for the daily stand-up. It should give one manager a clear, live picture of what the team is doing, where progress is being made and what is blocked. It should measure output and accountability, not hours or activity.

### Operating principles
- **Output over hours.** Completion and visible progress are the signals. Hours can be entered but are optional.
- **Deadlines are commitments.** Keep deadlines stable, make changes explicit with a recorded history, and flag risk early.
- **Unblock people, don't police them.** The app surfaces who is blocked or stalled so the manager can help. It does not track screens, idle time or keystrokes, and it never will.

## 3. Problem statement

- The manager had no single daily view of who did what, how loaded people were, or what was blocking progress.
- Updates given at stand-up or over chat were not searchable, not linked to projects, and left no history or metrics.
- Nothing held a member to what they said they would do. There was no promise and follow-up loop.
- Full project-management tools (Jira and similar) are too heavy for a small team's daily check-in.

### 5.3 What the data says

- **Two stages of use.** For the eight weeks up to 24 Aug, 1 to 3 members used the app, producing 52 submissions. From 31 Aug, 5 to 6 members a week used it, producing 147 submissions in six and a half weeks.
- **Submission rate.** For the six full weeks from 31 Aug to 10 Oct, there were 135 submissions out of a possible 252 member-days (7 members × 6 working days × 6 weeks). That is **about 54%**. The best week was 7 Sep, with 28 of 42 (67%). Two caveats apply:
  - Public holidays and leave are not modelled, so they count as missed days and pull the figure down.
  - Nothing stops two entries for the same person on the same day, which could push the figure up (section 12, item 2).
- **Usage is narrowing slightly.** One or two registered members are not active in a typical week. Submissions dipped from 24 to 25 a week in mid-September to 18 to 21 since then.

The manager may be getting the information somewhere else, such as the Today tab, the Sunday email or in person. This suggests the review step, or its 80% target, should be revisited rather than chased (section 17, question 7).

### 6.3 How data flows
1. **Login.**
   - Manager: the password is checked against Supabase Auth, and the Supabase session is then discarded.
   - Team member: the password is checked against the `employees` table.
   - In both cases the server signs the app's own session token into the cookie. The browser keeps a summary of the session in `sessionStorage`.
2. **Data calls.** Every tab calls `/api/...`. `proxy.ts` verifies the cookie, applies the manager-only rules, and passes the user's id, role and name to the route as headers. Routes use the Supabase service-role client for all database and storage work.
3. **Live refresh.** After a write, the browser sends a small "nudge" event over a Supabase Broadcast channel. Other open browsers re-fetch through the normal API. No row data travels over the socket. If a member is part-way through editing, refreshes wait so their form isn't overwritten.
4. **Weekly email.** Every Sunday, Vercel Cron calls `/api/cron/weekly-summary`. It rebuilds the summary of the week just ended from current data, stores it (replacing any copy saved earlier in the week), and emails it.

## 7. Functional requirements: authentication and session

- **FR-A1.** There is one login form. The manager's username (matched case-insensitively) is checked against Supabase Auth. Any other username is checked against the `employees` table.
- **FR-A2.** On success, the server sets a signed session cookie valid for 7 days. The user's role decides which interface loads.
- **FR-A3.** When the app loads, it checks the session saved in the browser by calling `/api/broadcast`. A 401 response clears it and shows the login page.
- **FR-A4.** Logout clears the cookie and the browser copy of the session. Tokens are not revoked on the server, so a copied token stays valid until it expires.
- **FR-A5.** Tokens are signed with `SESSION_SECRET`. If that is not set, the service-role key is used instead (risk R4).

## 8. Functional requirements: the commitments loop

Accountability comes from comparing what a member promised with what they delivered. Since v7, the app tracks only a weekly commitment. The daily commitment was removed because members almost always carried it forward, so it added work without adding signal.

**Step 1: follow up.** The open weekly commitment appears every day as a reminder. It never blocks the daily update. The member can choose:
- **Completed.** This closes it (`done`).
- **Partial** or **Carry Forward.** It stays open, moves to next week's Saturday, and `carry_count` goes up by one.
- **Edit.** This changes the wording. A reason is required, and it is saved as a dated line in `outcome_note`.
- **Cancel.** This drops a commitment that no longer applies. A reason is required. It is saved with status `cancelled` and is left out of reliability metrics.

**Deferred writes.** None of these actions is saved when clicked. They are held in the browser, shown as a pending banner with Undo, and saved only when the member submits that day's update. If the member leaves without submitting, the change is discarded.

**Step 2: log.** The member enters today's task rows (section 9.1) or marks themselves absent.

**Step 3: commit.** The rule is that a member always has exactly one open weekly commitment.
- A new weekly commitment, due this Saturday, is required when none would remain open after this submission. That happens when none is open, or when the open one is being completed or cancelled in this submission.
- No new commitment is required on a Saturday. The code applies this on any Saturday when none is open, not only on a first login of the week (correction, section 13).
- Each commitment shows its due date ("Complete by Saturday, DD Mon").

**Rules and signals**
- Weekly commitments are never carried automatically. They move only when the member chooses Partial or Carry Forward.
- **On-time delivery %** = commitments completed with `carry_count = 0`, divided by all completed commitments. Open and cancelled commitments are left out of both the top and bottom of the fraction. The same formula is used in the Commitments tab, My Stats, the weekly email and the CSV export.
- **Stalled** = open with `carry_count` of 3 or more. Stalled commitments appear as a list on the manager's Commitments tab and in the weekly email.
- **Server checks:** required fields, valid horizon, ownership, edit and carry only while the commitment is open, a reason required for edit and cancel, text up to 2,000 characters, at most 20 commitments in one batch. Only the manager can delete.
- Data from before v7 is kept. Old daily commitment rows remain in the database. The server still accepts `horizon = day`, but the interface never sends it.

## 9. Functional requirements: by role

### 9.1 Team member

**Daily update**
- **FR-E1.** The member adds one or more task rows. Each has a project (My Projects, Other Projects or Other Work), a title, optional hours, a status (In progress, Completed or Blocked), optional blocker text, a required "what changed since yesterday" field and optional attachments.
- **FR-E2.** Each status has its own icon: a navy ring for in progress, a green tick for completed, a red "!" for blocked.
- **FR-E3.** Attachments can be an image or file upload (images are compressed in the browser to at most 1600 px, JPEG quality 0.8, up to 2 MB) or a link. They open in a new tab.
- **FR-E4.** The member picks an overall workload: light, medium or heavy.
- **FR-E5.** Submission requires at least one task with a title, "what changed" on every task, and a weekly commitment where section 8 requires one. On submit, the entry is saved first, then the staged commitment actions, then the new weekly commitment.
- **FR-E5b.** Submit errors appear as a fixed toast message that the member can dismiss.

**Editing and lock**
- **FR-E6.** One update per day. It can be edited until the end of the IST day, after which the server refuses changes. This lock applies to edits only. Creating an entry for another date is not checked (section 12, item 1).
- **FR-E7.** Each edit increases `submit_count`. The manager sees an "edited" tag.
- **FR-E8.** Absent-day recovery: if the manager marked a member absent, the member sees a "Marked absent today" card with an "I worked today" button. Logging work clears the absence.
- **FR-E9.** Editing an entry does not create duplicate commitments.

**Views**
- **FR-E10.** A confirmation card after submitting, today's tasks, manager notes and open commitments.
- **FR-E11.** My Stats over 30 days: on-time delivery %, total updates, completion rate, hours, commitment outcomes, workload mix, task outcomes and project breakdown.
- **FR-E12.** Recent history: the last 5 entries before today, with manager notes and absences shown.
- **FR-E13.** The active broadcast banner, which can be dismissed.
- **FR-E14.** Live refresh when the manager changes something that affects the member.

**Self-service absence**
- **FR-E15.** A member can mark themselves absent for today with an optional reason.
- **FR-E16.** Marking absence skips the commitment requirement for that day.
- **FR-E17.** The reason is shown to the manager and included in the CSV export.

### 9.2 Manager

**Today**
- **FR-M1.** Stat cards for Submitted, Pending, Heavy, Medium and Light. Clicking a card filters the list.
- **FR-M2.** A "Yet to Submit" list with Absent and Submit-on-behalf actions for each member.
- **FR-M2b.** An "Absent Today" section that separates self-reported absences from absences the manager marked.
- **FR-M3.** Expanding an entry shows its tasks, what changed, attachments and blockers. The manager can mark it reviewed (or undo that) and add a note.
- **FR-M4.** Delete an entry. Edited entries show an "edited" tag.
- **FR-M5.** The manager can log their own daily update. This depends on an `employees` row with id `manager` existing, because of the foreign key. That has not been confirmed in production (section 12, item 12).
- **FR-M6.** Filter by member name.
- **FR-M7.** Live refresh when members submit or edit.

**Commitments**
- **FR-C1.** Team cards for On-time Delivery %, Commitments Made, Completed, Due/Overdue and Stalled. Cancelled commitments are excluded.
- **FR-C2.** A list of stalled commitments (carried 3 or more times).
- **FR-C3.** For each member: reliability, promised, delivered and open counts, and commitment history with notes. The period can be 7, 30 or 90 days.

**Blockers**
- **FR-B1.** Lists every task from the last 30 days that has status `blocked` or non-empty blocker text.
- **FR-B2.** Shows the age of each blocker, coloured grey on the day, orange after 1 day and red after 2 or more days.
- **FR-B3.** Resolve or reopen a blocker. It is identified by entry and task position.
- **FR-B4.** Show or hide resolved blockers.

**Projects**
- **FR-P1.** Create a project with a name, lead, members, start date, deadline and colour.
- **FR-P2.** Statistics for each project: today's count, total submissions, last activity, hours and contribution by member.
- **FR-P3.** Deadline alerts for projects that are overdue or due within 7 days.
- **FR-P4.** Set or change a deadline. Earlier deadlines are kept in `previous_deadlines`.
- **FR-P5.** Rename a project inline, close it (recording the end date) or delete it.
- **FR-P6.** Edit the team and lead. Members who have logged work on the project cannot be removed from it.
- **FR-P7.** A completed-projects section with on-time or missed-deadline badges.

**History and export**
- **FR-H1.** A Calendar view (the default) showing workload dots and the count of missing submissions per day.
- **FR-H2.** A Weekly view for each member, with day-by-day hours, project time, completion rate and CSV download.
- **FR-H3.** A People view: submission reliability (submitted ÷ Monday-to-Saturday working days), output, workload mix and projects, over a week, month, 3 months or all time.
- **FR-H4.** A List view with a member filter and CSV download.
- **FR-H5.** Quick date ranges of 7, 30 or 90 days.
- **FR-H6.** An Export dialog that produces a CSV with a member summary and daily task detail. Optional columns are flags, attachments, comments and commitments.

**Settings**
- **FR-S1.** Broadcast editor: message text and an on/off switch.
- **FR-S2.** The team table, with no passwords shown. Reset a password, or remove a member. Removing a member deletes all their entries and commitments (correction, section 13).
- **FR-S3.** Add a member with a name, username and password.

**Weekly Report**
- **FR-W1.** A list of the 26 most recent weekly summaries.
- **FR-W2.** Generate now: builds the summary for a chosen week and saves it. A saved summary is a snapshot. It does not update as new entries come in.
- **FR-W3.** Send now: rebuilds the summary from current data, saves it, then emails it to the manager, with a second address in CC. Recipients can be changed with environment variables.
- **FR-W4.** The Sunday job runs at 03:00 UTC (08:30 IST). It rebuilds the summary of the Monday-to-Saturday week just ended and sends it, so an earlier snapshot is never emailed. It skips any week that has already been sent.
- **FR-W5.** The email is built entirely from data, with no AI-written text. It contains: submission rate, commitments done and carried, on-time %, open blockers, a "needs attention" list (no activity, missed days, stalled commitments, blockers) and one card per member. Team members do not receive the email.

## 10. Security model and risk register

### 10.1 Controls in place
- Signed, `httpOnly` session cookie. A user cannot change their role from the browser.
- Central access rules in `proxy.ts`. Routes also check ownership.
- Team members can read and write only their own entries and commitments.
- Entry and commitment ids are random UUIDs, so they can't be guessed in sequence.
- Passwords are never returned to the browser. The manager can reset them but not read them.
- Uploads are limited by size and file type and stored under each user's own folder.
- Limits on request size: at most 50 tasks per entry and 20 commitments per batch.
- The scheduled job is protected by a secret.
- Realtime messages carry no data rows.
- No credentials have ever been committed to the application repository. A scan of the full git history found none.

### 10.2 Risk register
Effort: S = under 1 day, M = 1 to 3 days, L = more than 3 days.

| ID | Risk | Severity | Business impact | Fix | Effort |
|----|------|----------|-----------------|-----|--------|
| R1 | Row-level security is disabled on every table, while the public database key is shipped to the browser | Critical | Someone with technical knowledge could read or change tracker data without going through the app's access rules | Turn on row-level security with policies (or revoke public grants). Keep all data access on the server. | M |
| R2 | Team member passwords are stored and compared as plain text | Critical | Combined with R1, every password could be exposed. People who reuse passwords elsewhere would be affected too. | Hash passwords (bcrypt or argon2), migrate existing rows, ask members to reset | M |
| R3 | Service-role key and session secret have not been rotated since an earlier exposure risk. Legacy prototype files and the seed SQL file contain credential literals. | High | A leaked key gives full database control and the ability to forge logins | Rotate keys. Remove or clean the legacy files. Change the seeded passwords. | S |
| R4 | No dedicated session secret is set, so the service-role key signs sessions | High | Rotating the database key (R3) would log everyone out. If the key leaks, logins can be forged. | Set a dedicated `SESSION_SECRET` | S |
| R5 | The manager login is hard-coded to one external auth account | High | A new environment or an auth problem would lock the manager out. Only one manager is possible. | Make the manager identity configurable and document how to set it up | M |
| R6 | Manager notes can be read by any logged-in user who has an entry id | Medium | One member could read notes written about another member | Limit comment reads to the entry's owner and the manager | S |
| R7 | Uploaded files are publicly readable by URL | Medium | Screenshots could be viewed by anyone who has the link | Use a private bucket with signed URLs | M |
| R8 | No login rate limiting, CSRF token or security headers | Medium | Passwords can be guessed by brute force. Weaker protection against browser-based attacks. | Add rate limiting and lockout, CSRF protection and standard headers | M |
| R9 | No audit log (apart from commitment edit and cancel reasons) | Medium | Cannot show who changed what | Add an audit table for changes | M |
| R10 | The weekly email depends on a personal Gmail app password | Low | It has already failed once (26 Aug) when the password stopped working | Use a team mail account or a transactional email service. Keep the rotation runbook in the README. | S |
| R11 | Logout does not revoke sessions on the server | Low | A copied cookie keeps working for up to 7 days | Add a session version or a deny-list | S |

### 10.3 Incidents
- **26 Aug 2026, weekly email failing.** Gmail rejected the SMTP login. The app password was regenerated and updated in production. This was a configuration fix with no code change.
- **Sep 2026, two weekly commitments cancelled by mistake.** Testing was done against a browser that still had the build from before deferred writes, so Cancel saved immediately. The two rows were restored with a one-off script, which was then deleted. The code was working as intended. A hard refresh fixed the stale build.
- **25 Oct 2026, weekly email sent with all zeros.** The email for 19 to 24 Oct showed 0% submission and "no updates" for every member, although 19 entries had been logged. A summary for that week had been generated on Monday 19 Oct at 3:47 pm, before anyone had submitted. The Sunday job found that saved copy and sent it as it was, instead of rebuilding it. What created the Monday copy is unknown, because there is no audit log (R9). The data was not affected. The summary was regenerated the same evening and showed 45%. Fix: every send now rebuilds the summary first (commit `dd33b10`, tag `TeamTrackingV2.3`), with a unit test for this case.

## 12. Known issues and edge cases (from the 15 Oct audit)

These were all confirmed in the code. None of them is known to have caused a problem in production.

| # | Issue | Effect | Fix |
|---|-------|--------|-----|
| 1 | Creating an entry does not check its date | A member can create entries for past or future dates. The end-of-day lock only covers edits. | Reject any date other than today on the server, except for the manager |
| 2 | Nothing prevents two entries for the same person on the same day | The weekly email counts rows, so duplicates inflate the submission rate | Add a unique (employee_id, date) constraint after cleaning existing data |
| 3 | Submitting is not atomic: the entry, then each commitment action, then the new commitment are saved one at a time | A failure part-way leaves partial data | Move the submit into a single server-side transaction |
| 4 | The entry timestamp is overwritten on every edit | The original submission time is lost | Add a `first_submitted_at` column |
| 5 | Blocker resolution is keyed by task position | Reordering or removing tasks the same day attaches the resolution to the wrong task | Give each task a stable id |
| 6 | "Today" is calculated once when some tabs load | Leaving the app open past midnight IST shows stale dates | Recalculate it when the tab regains focus |
| 7 | 30-day windows use UTC dates | The window can be off by one day | Use the IST date helpers |
| 8 | Carrying a commitment changes its due date | Regenerating an old week's summary loses commitments that were carried out of that week. Since 25 Oct, Send now also regenerates, so re-sending an old week has the same effect. | Record weekly snapshots, or a log of carry events |
| 9 | The CSV export counts cancelled commitments; the Commitments tab does not | The two show different totals | Exclude cancelled commitments in the export |
| 10 | CSV cells are not protected against spreadsheet formulas | A task title starting with `=` could run as a formula in Excel | Prefix risky cells with an apostrophe |
| 11 | Manager notes can be read across members | See R6 | See R6 |
| 12 | The manager's own entries need an `employees` row with id `manager` | If the row is missing, the manager's own updates fail to save | Confirm the row exists, or relax the foreign key for the manager |
| 13 | Holidays and leave are not modelled | They count as missed days in the submission rate and trigger "missed days" in the email | Add a holiday and leave calendar |
| 14 | Logout leaves sessions valid | See R11 | See R11 |
| 15 | Removing a member deletes their history | All their entries, commitments and the notes on them are lost | Archive members (soft delete) instead |

Housekeeping:
- The browser keeps the session in `sessionStorage`, so members have to log in again in each new tab even though the cookie is still valid.
- `lib/ai.ts` (the Gemini narrative) is no longer used by any feature.
- `@supabase/ssr` is installed but not used.
- The six migration files must be run in exact order on a new environment.

## 15. Roadmap (proposed, in priority order)

Each phase is ordered by risk first, then value. No dates are set. Effort codes are as in section 10.2.

### Phase 1: Security hardening (do first)
Goal: close the critical and high risks before the tool holds more history or more people.
- Turn on row-level security, or revoke public grants, on all tables (R1).
- Hash passwords and have all members reset them (R2).
- Set a dedicated session secret, then rotate the service-role key (R4, R3).
- Remove credential literals from the legacy HTML files and the seed SQL (R3).
- Limit manager notes to the entry's owner and the manager (R6).
- Login rate limiting and lockout (R8).

Exit criteria: no critical or high risk remains open in section 10.2.

### Phase 2: Data integrity and quality
Goal: make the numbers in section 5 trustworthy and keep them that way.
- Check entry dates on the server, add a one-entry-per-day constraint, and give each task a stable id (issues 1, 2, 5).
- Make submit a single transaction (issue 3).
- Archive members instead of deleting them (issue 15).
- Make CSV export safe from formulas and consistent with the app's counts (issues 9, 10).
- Fix the 12 lint errors. Add CI that runs lint, type-check and tests on every push.
- Add API and auth tests for the commitment rules and access control.
- Merge the six migrations into one ordered, re-runnable set.

Exit criteria: lint and tests pass in CI, and all of section 12, items 1 to 5, 9, 10 and 15, are closed.

### Phase 3: Adoption and manager workflow
Goal: raise the submission rate from about 54% towards the 90% target, and make the manager's review step match how it is really used.
- Holiday and leave calendar, so rates and alerts stop counting days off (issue 13).
- Reminder to members who have not submitted, in the app first and by email later.
- Daily manager digest, built as an in-app view first.
- Rework the review step: a bulk "mark all reviewed", or count the weekly email as the review. Then decide on a realistic target (question 7).
- Remember the session across tabs (no re-login in each new tab).
- Fix the Projects tab loading every entry since 2024, and add pagination.

Exit criteria: submission rate measured with holidays removed, and the reminder live.

### Phase 4: Work items and engagement
- Work items that persist across days, with multiple assignees and dependencies or hand-offs between members.
- Deadline integrity: original and current deadline, a reason for each change, and an at-risk flag.
- An engagement view framed as blocked, waiting or stalled.
- Audit log (R9). Private attachments with signed URLs (R7).

### Phase 5: Zoho Projects integration
- Feasibility spike covering OAuth, scopes and how tracker projects and work items map to Zoho.
- One-way push of status, progress and daily notes as Zoho task comments.
- A mapping screen and sync status.

The data model has been kept ready for Zoho since v6, with stable ids and a status list that maps to Zoho statuses, so this phase can be added on top rather than requiring a rewrite.

## 17. Open questions

1. **Passwords.** Hash them in the current table, or move team members to Supabase Auth? Moving would also solve R5 (the hard-coded manager login).
2. **Holiday and leave source.** Should holidays be maintained by the manager in the app, or taken from an HR calendar?
3. **Who counts as active.** Two registered members are often inactive. Are they on other duties, or should they be removed from the denominator, or followed up?
4. **At-risk deadline rule.** What should trigger the flag? For example: fewer than X days left and no progress in Y days.
5. **Hours field.** Keep it as an optional light signal, or remove it?
6. **Attachment retention.** How long should files be kept, now that storage grows without limit?
7. **Manager review.** Is the "mark reviewed" step still wanted? If it is, what target is realistic? If it isn't, should the Sunday email count as the review and the 80% target be dropped?
8. **AI code.** Delete the unused `lib/ai.ts`, or keep it for a possible AI summary later?
9. **Hosting plan.** Confirm whether Vercel and Supabase are on free tiers, and what the storage and database limits are.

## Appendix B. Glossary
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Submission | A daily update that is not an absence |
| Weekly commitment | One promise per member per week, due on Saturday |
| Carry forward | Moving an unfinished commitment to next week's Saturday. Adds 1 to `carry_count`. |
| On-time delivery % | Commitments completed without ever being carried, divided by all completed commitments (cancelled ones excluded) |
| Stalled | An open commitment carried 3 or more times |
| Working day | Monday to Saturday, IST. Holidays are not yet modelled. |
| Row-level security (RLS) | A database feature that limits which rows each caller can access |
